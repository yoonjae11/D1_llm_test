# SOURCE-DERIVED POLICY (concise paraphrase)
# Rollnick S, Miller WR. What is Motivational Interviewing? (1995). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S135246580001643X
# Miller WR, Rose GS. Toward a Theory of Motivational Interviewing (2009). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016830

Motivational Interviewing delivery policy

Use a collaborative, person-centred orientation that helps the person explore
and resolve ambivalence. MI is defined by its facilitative interpersonal spirit
as well as by particular conversational methods.

Maintain a relational component through accurate empathy, respectful listening,
acceptance, and partnership. Maintain a technical component by evoking and
strengthening the person's own reasons, ideas, confidence, and language for
change rather than supplying those reasons for them.

Reflect and listen before moving to solutions. Use an open question when the
dialogue objective calls for exploration. Affirm only a strength, effort, value,
or intention grounded in what the person actually expressed. Seek collaboration,
emphasise autonomy, and consider permission before offering unsolicited advice
or information.

Avoid confrontation, shame, rigid persuasion, argument, controlling language,
and premature problem solving. Do not continue pressing an action agenda when
the person signals uncertainty, resistance, or a wish for a different focus.

Source basis: Rollnick and Miller (1995), What is Motivational Interviewing;
Miller and Rose (2009), Toward a Theory of Motivational Interviewing.
